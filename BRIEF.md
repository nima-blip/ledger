\# Ledger — Brief



\## Who it is for



Its for people like me who want to improve the productivity in their lives by creating an anchor plan around subject matters like health, food, learning, work, sport etc but are conscious that plans should be dynamic and need to be adapted









\## The problem in their words



The problem is that plans are often too ambitious, become stale and users become demotivated. They do not adapt to the where the user actually "is" mentally and physically from day to day and so users just break from the plan and dont revert to it.









\## The three things it must do

1\. Take a five-minute voice check-in and log it, and build a plan for the next day using the anchor information, and the detail in the voice log

2\. Hold the week's plan and re-flow it when a day is missed.

3\. Tell me what's next without being asked.





\## What it will not do



1. Not diagnose or give medical advice — it recommends, cites, and points at a person.
2. Not be a calendar; it reads one later, it isn’t one.
3. Not require typing — if logging takes more than ten seconds a day, it’s failed.
4. Not nag, badge, streak or gamify.
5. Not a social network; Social is a log of who you saw, not a feed.
6. Not count calories or weigh food; Food is “what did you eat, how did it sit”, not a diet app.
7. Not replace a coach, a therapist or a physio.
8. Not a general chatbot — Ask answers from your own data, not the internet.
9. Not free — no ad-supported tier.
10. Not a podcast player; it picks the episode, you play it elsewhere.
11. Not sell or share your data, ever.







\## How we will know it works

1. I log five days a week for four weeks running, by voice, without being reminded — by mid-December.
2. Logging takes under ten seconds a day; the check-in under five minutes.
3. The Friday review takes under 15 minutes because the week’s data is already in it — by January.
4. When I miss a day, the next morning’s plan is right without me editing it — by Week 4.
5. I follow its Episode Picker suggestion more often than not — by February.
6. Three people who aren’t me have used it for two weeks and one has paid — by June.
7. No data leaves the system; the pen test in Week 23 finds nothing critical.







\## The four risks



Value: Users genuinely feel more productive after using this

Usability: The biggest unknown is the voice-first front door itself: can someone talk to it like a mate, unprompted, and have it understand — or does it need a script? Most voice products fail here; people freeze, don’t know what to say, and the app gets nothing useful. Test: hand it to Megan with no explanation and see if she completes a check-in; and count how many times you yourself skip it because it felt like effort. If you’re skipping by Week 6, usability is the problem, not motivation.

Feasibility: the unknown is the voice check-in: can speech-to-text plus a model reliably turn five minutes of rambling into clean log entries? Test: Week 11’s speech-to-text comparison on your own recordings.

Viability: The unknown is cost per user: every check-in calls a model and a transcription service, so does a subscription cover it? Test: measure the API cost per day of use in Week 13 and compare to a plausible monthly price.
